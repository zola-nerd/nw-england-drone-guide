UK drone rules explained

Disclaimer: This is an independent guide written by a hobby drone pilot. It is general information, not legal advice, and it isn't connected to the CAA. Rules change, so always check the official CAA drone pages, airspace tools and landowner rules before you fly.

Flyer ID

The CAA says you need a Flyer ID to fly a drone weighing 100g or more. You get one by passing a free online theory test, and it lasts five years. Teenagers aged 13 to 17 can take the test on their own. Children under 13 need a parent or guardian with them.

Official page: CAA – Flyer IDs and Operator IDs

Operator ID

An Operator ID is needed for drones weighing 250g or more, or 100g or more with a camera. The ID number must be labelled on the drone. You must be 18 or over to register, so if you're under 18 a parent or guardian registers as the operator. The CAA lists an annual fee, so check the current price on its site.

Official page: CAA – Children and parent guidance

Class marks

New drone models sold from 1 January 2026 must carry a UK class mark (UK0 to UK6). Older drones can be flown according to their European C class mark or their weight. The class mark tells you which Open sub-category you can fly in.

Official page: CAA – Class marks

The Drone Code

Read the full official Drone and Model Aircraft Code. It covers height limits, distance from people, and where you can and can't fly.
